# Q4 frontier and forecast

Generated by `scripts/q4_evolution_run.py`. Do not edit by hand.

## Definitions

- Frontier: the conditional 90th percentile of the macro score, by linear quantile regression on
  calendar time (level at the anchor and trend). Fixed before any forecast was evaluated; not tuned.
- Descriptive frontier: the monthly 90th percentile, reported only for months with at least 20 models
  (smaller months are flagged sparse). Sensitivities: the monthly maximum and top-10 mean.
- **Anchor: 2025-03-13**, the final observed date of the primary population.
- **12-month primary horizon: 2026-03-13. 24-month stress horizon: 2027-03-13** (stress / longer-horizon
  extrapolation; never co-equal evidence).

Three kinds of forecast number, never interchangeable (T-011 v3):

- **historical / direct-score continuation**: the direct frontier time trend (the primary descriptive forecast
  trend) continued from the anchor. It is a historical baseline, not an answer to a compute slowdown.
- **parameter-scale-growth scenarios**: the continuation trend split by the post-failure frontier-set
  decomposition, with the parameter-count (log N) component multiplied by `param_scale_half` = 0.5, `param_scale_frozen` = 0.
  Parameter count is not training compute, so these are not compute-growth scenarios. A slower
  parameter-scale growth only removes a positive parameter-scale component; where that component is
  not positive the scenario is non-binding and equals the continuation.
- **assumption-based transferred compute-slowdown sensitivity**: the problem's compute-slowdown request. The frontier trend
  is split by the compute share estimated on the stratum-A C4 compute subset and that share is
  slowed. Because representative historical compute is unavailable for this frontier, the compute-slowdown forecast transfers the compute-associated share estimated on the small pretrained C4 subset. It is therefore a scenario sensitivity, not an identified population effect.

The non-scale part of every number is assumed to continue linearly over the horizon: an assumption,
not a finding; nothing supports it beyond the observed window.
