**Disposition.** IF4 is a degenerate, no-slope bridge over a narrow primary support: it records the
benchmark floor and its out-of-fold prediction error, and nothing more. It identifies no
transferable loss-to-score slope, pools no cross-family losses, and is not a conversion law usable
for frontier forecasting. That it validates against the shared schema adds no scientific weight.

## Bridge-based translation

A bridge-based score needs a loss on the IF4 scale inside the IF4 support; the loss of a real
model comes from the classic IF3, which is accepted only inside its box.

- compute-bearing C4 rows with inferred (N, D) inside the IF3 box: 6; of these, non-anchor rows whose IF3 loss lies inside the IF4 support: 2

| Model | IF3 loss | Bridge score | Bridge band SD | Observed macro |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `microsoft/phi-1` | 2.4475 | 5.66 | 0.42 | 5.57 |
| `microsoft/phi-1_5` | 2.3187 | 5.66 | 0.42 | 7.17 |

Band SD = root sum of squares of the IF4 predictive SD and the propagated loss uncertainty.
The loss uncertainty is the classic IF3's observed external error (B4 with Pythia excluded,
median abs. rel. error 0.0807, scaled to an SD by 1.4826); the B1 bootstrap intervals are
not used because they measure generator recovery, not real-world precision. With a constant bridge the loss uncertainty contributes nothing to the score, so the IF4 error is the whole band: bridge uncertainty dominates.

**Frontier translation is not admissible.** Of the 55 compute-bearing rows with an inferred D,
33 score more than three bridge SDs above the anchors' highest score; 33 of those lie outside the IF3 box and 29 have an
(extrapolated) IF3 loss below the IF4 support minimum 2.0933. The BC frontier set's median size is
14.77B parameters against a box maximum of 11.97B. Wherever the benchmark
moves off its floor, a bridge-based score would need an IF3 extrapolation and a bridge extrapolation
together, so none is produced: every frontier forecast in `q4-frontier-forecast.md` is direct
score-based.
