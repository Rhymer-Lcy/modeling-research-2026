# Q2 marginal effects and iso-loss substitution under the candidate form

Generated by `scripts/q2_substitution.py`. Do not edit by hand.

**Candidate** form: `L(N,D,Q) = E + A N^(-alpha) + B (Q^gamma D)^(-beta)`,
with N in billions of parameters and D in billions of tokens, as stored
in the source table.

> **Status of this document.** `(Q^gamma * D)` is a CANDIDATE form. It is
> **not adopted**, and no generalized IF3 has been emitted. The audit in
> `q2-quality-data-audit.md` shows the fitted form **does not reproduce
> B7 to that file's storage precision** (fingerprint ratio in the
> hundreds, not near 1).
>
> What that supports is narrow, and is stated narrowly: the form is **not
> B7's exact recovered generator**, and the fitted gamma is an
> **imperfect approximation under the current fit** rather than a
> recovered constant, obtained on a single designed, unreplicated,
> semi-synthetic grid. It is a reproduction-accuracy statement - no noise
> model is posited and no hypothesis is tested - so it does **not**
> amount to a statistical rejection of the functional form.
>
> This document therefore contains two different kinds of statement, and
> they are kept apart throughout:
>
> * **Analytical identities** - exact consequences of the candidate form
>   itself, true for any parameters in the valid region, independent of
>   any dataset. The signs and the substitution algebra are of this kind
>   and are machine-checked.
> * **Numerical illustrations** - values obtained by substituting the B7
>   fit into those identities. These are **model-internal, illustrative
>   sensitivity only**. They are **NOT the final Q2 empirical
>   substitution result**, and must not be quoted as a measured
>   property of real models.

## Analytical identities (independent of any dataset)

These follow from the candidate form alone and hold for every parameter
vector in the valid region (A, B, alpha, beta, gamma all positive). They
do not depend on the B7 fit, and are unaffected by how closely that fit
reproduces B7.

* `dL/dN < 0`, `dL/dD < 0`, `dL/dQ < 0`.
* Along a curve of constant loss, `dN/dQ = -L_Q / L_N`. Both partials are
  negative, so `dN/dQ < 0`: raising quality **lowers** the parameter count
  needed for the same loss. Reporting this positive is the standard error.
* Parameter saving `[N^(-alpha) + R/A]^(-1/alpha) - N` is negative and
  always finite.
* Equivalent capacity `[N^(-alpha) - R/A]^(-1/alpha) - N` is positive and
  finite only while `R < A N^(-alpha)`, diverging at that boundary.

All of the above are machine-checked in `scripts/selftest_quality.py`,
including against a deliberately invalid law.

### Elasticities

Three quantities that are easy to conflate, reported separately.
`dlogL` is the elasticity of TOTAL loss, damped by the irreducible floor
E. `dlogLex` is the elasticity of EXCESS (reducible) loss, `L - E`, which
is the quantity that equals `-alpha` in the pure power-law limit. They
are not interchangeable and the total-loss version is always smaller in
magnitude.

### Parameter saving and equivalent capacity

Two different questions about the same quality improvement, which are
routinely conflated:

* **Parameter saving** `[N^(-alpha) + R/A]^(-1/alpha) - N` - how many
  parameters can now be *removed* while holding loss constant. Always
  negative and always finite.
* **Equivalent capacity** `[N^(-alpha) - R/A]^(-1/alpha) - N` - how many
  parameters would have been needed to buy the same gain *instead*.
  Positive, and finite only while `R < A N^(-alpha)`; it diverges at that
  boundary, which is a result and not a numerical failure.

Here R is the fall in the data/quality term produced by raising Q from
its base value to the target, holding D fixed.

| N (B) | D (B) | Q base | Q target | R | Parameter saving (B) | Equivalent capacity (B) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.41 | 50 | 0.5 | 0.55 | 0.013225 | -0.026805 | 0.029241 |
| 0.41 | 50 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 0.055543 | -0.098861 | 0.14261 |
| 0.41 | 50 | 0.5 | 1 | 0.093833 | -0.14979 | 0.27928 |
| 2.8 | 150 | 0.7 | 0.77 | 0.01191 | -0.27302 | 0.31174 |
| 2.8 | 150 | 0.7 | 1 | 0.044091 | -0.85855 | 1.4068 |
| 11.97 | 600 | 0.9 | 0.99 | 0.010587 | -1.5136 | 1.8041 |
| 11.97 | 600 | 0.9 | 1 | 0.011698 | -1.658 | 2.0131 |

Every quality target above lies inside the fitted validity box Q in [0.1, 1]; targets are clipped to
that ceiling and de-duplicated, and the script raises rather than print a
target outside it.

Where the equivalent capacity is reported as having no finite value, the
quality improvement removes more loss than the parameter term can supply
at that N: no parameter count, however large, buys the same gain. That
is stated rather than hidden behind a blank cell.

## Verification

Every formula above is checked by `scripts/selftest_quality.py`, which
compares each analytic partial against a central finite difference,
asserts the three monotonicity signs and the negative iso-loss slope,
checks that the two substitution transforms exactly restore the loss they
claim to, requires the equivalent-capacity boundary to raise rather than
return NaN, and finally feeds a deliberately invalid law (negative
gamma) to confirm the sign checks can fail.
