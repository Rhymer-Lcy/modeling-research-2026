# Q2 quality-bearing data: structural and provenance audit

Generated by `scripts/q2_audit_quality_data.py`. Do not edit by hand.

Run before any quality law is believed. A fit to a generated table
recovers that table's generator, which looks exactly like a law that
works. This audit separates the two so the distinction survives into
every downstream claim.

## Structure

| Table | Rows | N levels | D levels | Q levels | N range (B params) | D range (B tokens) | Q range | Duplicate (N,D,Q) | Grid |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| B6 | 360 | 9 | 5 | 8 | 0.07 - 11.97 | 10 - 600 | 0.1 - 1 | 0 | complete |
| B7 | 450 | 9 | 5 | 10 | 0.07 - 11.97 | 10 - 600 | 0.1 - 1 | 0 | complete |
| B8 | 1704 | 15 | 10 | 12 | 0.07 - 700 | 5 - 2000 | 0.05 - 1 | 0 | 96 missing |

All three are full or near-full factorial grids over (N, D, Q) with no
repeated design point. A designed grid with exactly one loss per cell
carries no replication, so it cannot express measurement noise: there is
no within-cell variance to estimate. That is a property of a generated
table, not of a training campaign.

## Relationship between the tables

| Pair | Shared (N,D,Q) keys | Identical loss | Max abs. difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| B6 vs B7 | 360 | 360 | 0 |
| B6 vs B8 | 160 | 0 | 2.577 |
| B7 vs B8 | 224 | 0 | 2.577 |

**B7 contains B6 exactly.** Every B6 design point appears in B7 with a
bit-identical loss (superset on keys: True; maximum
difference over the shared cells is 0). B7 adds two further quality
levels and nothing else. They are therefore **one table, not two**, and
must never be pooled or counted as independent evidence.

**B8 is a different generator.** It shares design points with both, yet
agrees with neither on a single one of them, and its loss range does not
even overlap theirs at the low end. B8 cannot be pooled with B6/B7.

## Direction of the quality effect - the decisive finding

Before fitting anything, the sign of `d logL / d logQ` is measured in
**every** (N, D) cell of every table, not in cells chosen by hand.

| Table | Cells | Slope negative | Slope positive | Median slope | Min | Max |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| B6 | 45 | 45 | 0 | -0.05371 | -0.07561 | -0.02845 |
| B7 | 45 | 45 | 0 | -0.05174 | -0.07572 | -0.02793 |
| B8 calibrated | 90 | 0 | 90 | 0.5521 | 0.3543 | 0.6385 |
| B8 extrapolated | 60 | 0 | 60 | 0.5318 | 0.4717 | 0.6349 |

**The sign is unanimous within each table and opposite between them.**
In B6 and B7 loss falls as `Q_score` rises, in every single cell. In both
B8 strata loss *rises* as `Q_score` rises, in every single cell, and by
an order of magnitude larger slope.

On the 224 design points B7 and B8 share, B7's median loss falls from
2.66 at Q=0.1 to lower values as Q climbs, while B8's climbs from 0.50.

**Consequence.** `Q_score` does not carry the same meaning in B8 as in
B6/B7. Under the candidate law a positive `dL/dQ` is not representable at
all - the form requires quality to reduce loss - so fitting B8 does not
estimate a quality exponent. It drives gamma to its lower bound and
returns a number with no interpretation. B8 is therefore **excluded from
quality-law estimation** until its column semantics are resolved, rather
than folded in to enlarge the sample.

Reading the meaning of `Q_score` off its name is exactly the error this
check exists to catch: the name is identical in all three tables and the
behaviour is not.

## Loss floor

| Table | Minimum loss | Rows at that minimum |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| B6 | 2.016 | 1 |
| B7 | 2.016 | 1 |
| B8 calibrated | 0.5 | 129 |
| B8 extrapolated | 0.5 | 233 |

A minimum repeated across many rows is a clamp, not a coincidence: the
generator floors its output. Rows sitting on a clamp carry no gradient
information and would bias any fit that included them.

## Generator-recovery fingerprint

Fitted only for the tables whose quality effect runs in the direction the
law can represent: B6, B7.

Each table is fitted with the quality-aware law and the residuals are
compared with the rounding quantum implied by the decimal places the
source file stores. A ratio near 1 means the fit reproduces the table to
its own storage precision.

| Table | n | gamma | alpha | beta | Median abs. rel. err. | Rounding quantum | Ratio |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| B6 | 360 | 1.2088 | 0.2791 | 0.059172 | 0.01786 | 1.939e-05 | **920.9** |
| B7 | 450 | 1.1887 | 0.27404 | 0.069882 | 0.01681 | 1.942e-05 | **865.3** |

The ratios are in the hundreds, not near 1. So the candidate form does
**not** reproduce B6/B7 to their storage precision either: it leaves a
median relative residual of order 1.7%, against a rounding quantum of
order 2e-05. Unlike the classic N-D law on the principal table, this is
*not* a generator recovery - the supplied quality mechanism is not the
`(Q^gamma * D)` effective-token form. The fitted gamma is a
best-approximation under a misspecified form, and is reported as such.

## B8 strata kept separate

B8 labels its own rows `calibrated` (984) and
`extrapolated` (720). Merging them would present a
model's own extrapolation as if it were calibration data.

The cross-stratum check that would normally follow - fit the calibrated
stratum, predict the extrapolated one - is **not reported**, because the
quality-law fit that check depends on is not meaningful for B8 under the
inverted Q direction established above. Reporting a number from it would
dress a degenerate fit as a validation.

## B9 / B10 role audit

| Property | B9 `supplementary_large_models.csv` | B10 `supplementary_large_baseline.csv` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rows | 132 | 128 |
| Carries a loss column | no | yes |
| Carries a quality column | no | no |
| Columns | `model_name`, `N_params_B`, `D_tokens_B`, `FLOPs`, `publication_date`, `organization`, `accessibility`, `country` | `family`, `N_params_B`, `D_tokens_B`, `val_loss`, `is_converged` |
| N range (B params) | 100 - 1e+04 | 100 - 1e+04 |
| D range (B tokens) | 0 - 3.6e+04 | 0.1 - 3.6e+04 |

Joining on model name: 128 matched, 4 in B9 only, 0 in B10 only.

Present in B9 with no B10 loss: `DLRM-2020`, `InstructGPT 175B`, `LMSI-Palm`, `Med-PaLM 2`.

**Roles.** B9 is a *specification* of real large models - identity, size,
token count, compute, publication date, organisation - and carries no
loss at all, so it can never be validation data. B10 supplies a loss for
almost all of them, and that loss is model output, not measurement.
Fitting to B10 and then reporting agreement with B10 would be circular;
comparing an independent fit against it can only bound disagreement
between two estimates.

**Neither carries Q.** The quality-aware law therefore cannot be
evaluated on the large-model set without a quality value supplied from
outside, which is an IF1 dependency, not something recoverable here.

## What this audit establishes

1. B6 and B7 are the same table; only B7 should be used.
2. **B8's `Q_score` runs opposite to B6/B7's**, unanimously across all
   150 of its cells. The three tables do not share a meaning for that
   column. B8 is excluded from quality-law estimation until its
   semantics are resolved; it is not pooled, and not reported as
   corroboration.
3. The candidate `(Q^gamma * D)` effective-token form does **not**
   reproduce B6/B7 to storage precision. gamma is therefore a
   best-approximation under a form the data do not support exactly, not
   a recovered generator constant.
4. Every quality-bearing table is a designed, unreplicated grid. Any
   quality exponent from them describes the supplied mechanism and must
   not be reported as empirical evidence that real models obey it.
5. Large sample counts here do not strengthen evidence. B8's 1704 rows
   are design points, not observations - and are currently unusable for
   this purpose regardless of their number.
6. B9 is a specification and B10 is generator output; neither is
   independent validation, and neither carries Q.

## Escalation

Finding 2 is a stop condition for the quality-law subtask as specified:
the actual structure of the quality tables contradicts the assumption
that they share a quality scale. The resolution requires knowing what
B8's `Q_score` measures - it may be a corruption or noise fraction, a
differently-normalised score, or a different quantity altogether. That
is a provenance question, not something recoverable by fitting, and it
is recorded rather than guessed.
